Cost of Living (cross-border guide)

Cost of Living — free tools and expert guides for cross-border workers (frontalieri) between Switzerland and Italy. Compare salaries, tax, LAMal health insurance, pensions, and cost of living in Ticino. Updated 2026.

By Frontaliere Ticino Editorial Team

Living costs in Switzerland and Italy vary by city, housing and household size, so there is no single saving percentage for everyone. In Eurostat’s 2025 household-consumption price-level index (EU average=100), Switzerland is about 171 and Italy about 100. This national purchasing-power comparison does not compare rents in Lugano and Como and does not mean every Swiss expense is 71% higher. See Eurostat data.

To compare living in Italy with living in Ticino, calculate rent, groceries, health coverage and commuting separately. Our cost-of-living comparator lets you enter your situation; the net salary calculator helps estimate disposable income. Results depend on the inputs and are not tax advice.

A G or B permit does not by itself determine your tax treatment. The 2020 Italy–Switzerland agreement took effect on 17 July 2023 and distinguishes new cross-border workers from those eligible for the transitional regime. Check the requirements and tax-credit rules in the official Swiss tax authority guidance, then factor your route into the border-wait tool.

Content is designed to help cross-border workers make informed decisions about taxation, pensions, transportation, cost of living, and administrative procedures.

All tools and data are updated for the 2026 fiscal year, reflecting the New Bilateral Tax Agreement between Switzerland and Italy, current AVS/LPP contribution rates, and Canton Ticino withholding tax tables.

The platform covers the complete cross-border worker lifecycle: from obtaining your G or B permit and opening a Swiss bank account, to filing your annual tax returns in both countries, planning your AVS and LPP pension, and comparing the cost of living on both sides of the border.

Frequently asked questions

How much higher are prices in Switzerland than in Italy?
In the 2025 Eurostat index of price levels for household consumption (EU average=100), Switzerland is about 171 and Italy about 100. It is a national index based on purchasing power parities, not a comparison of rents in Lugano and Como or an estimate of a household budget. Source: https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/view/tec00120/default/table?lang=en
Is it worth living in Italy and working in Switzerland?
For many cross-border workers yes: the Swiss salary combined with the Italian cost of living allows a higher net saving, especially on rent and groceries. However, you should factor in transport costs, travel time and the applicable tax regime.
Which towns near the border have the lowest cost of living?
There is no single ranking that fits every household. Rents and expenses depend on the neighbourhood, the size of the home, the period and the links to your workplace. Compare current listings and transport costs for the municipalities you are considering.
How much does it cost to live in Lugano or Como?
The cost depends on housing, household, health insurance and habits. The Eurostat index compares national price levels, but it provides neither a monthly budget nor rents for individual cities. Use current figures and compare equivalent items.
How do I compare a rent in Chiasso or Mendrisio?
Compare listings available in the same period for homes of similar size and condition, including ancillary costs, deposit and distance from work. Prices change over time and between neighbourhoods; check the terms stated in the listing and in the contract.
