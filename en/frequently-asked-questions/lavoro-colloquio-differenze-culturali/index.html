What are the main cultural differences in Ticino job interviews? (cross-border guide)

Answered with official sources — Job search and CV

Home / Cross-border guide / 103 FAQs for Italian cross-border workers in Ticino / Job search and CV

Updated 8 August 2026

Answer

Ticino interviews blend Swiss formality with Latin warmth. Absolute punctuality (5 minutes early minimum), formal attire for banks/insurance, business casual elsewhere. Firm handshake, eye contact. Avoid excess familiarity: formal «you» until invited to first-name basis (rare in first interview). Questions are direct but polite: experience, motivation, company knowledge, availability. Do not denigrate past employers. Salary openness is normal: give a realistic range based on the CLA. Always bring paper CV copy, prior Arbeitszeugnis, diplomas, references. Thank-you email within 24 hours is appreciated. Average response time 2-4 weeks source: Ticino Chamber of Commerce interview guide.

Other questions on this topic

  • Can cross-border workers take part in Swiss public competitions?

    Yes, with constraints. The Confederation and Cantons apply the AFMP non-discrimination principle (Annex I art. 9) for EU/EFTA citizens.

  • What are the notice periods in Swiss employment contracts?

    CO art. 335c sets minimum notice periods: 7 days during trial period (typically 1-3 months at start, CO art. 335b), 1 month in year 1, 2 months years 2-9, 3 months from year 10. CLAs may extend (e.g. 6 months for bank executives).

  • Which languages are needed to work in Ticino?

    Italian is the main language (Ticino Constitution art. 1). Commercial, financial and hospitality roles require German B2-C1 (banks, private banking), French is appreciated. English is nearly always required at B2+ in multinationals, pharma (e.g. Roche Mendrisio) and tech.

  • Does a fixed-term contract give right to a G permit?

    Yes, the G permit is also issued for fixed-term contracts (CTD), for the contract duration up to 5 years. Contracts up to 3 months require only online notification on EasyGov without physical permit (simplified AFMP art. 9 Annex I procedure).

  • How do I get my Italian diploma recognised in Switzerland?

    Recognition depends on profession. For regulated professions (doctor, nurse, lawyer, architect, teacher) SERI (State Secretariat for Education, Research and Innovation) issues equivalence under EU Directive 2005/36/EC.

  • Can I register with the Swiss URC as a cross-border worker?

    Registration as an employed worker: no. The URC only helps persons registered as Swiss unemployed, i.e. Swiss residents. Italian-resident cross-border job seekers can freely consult arbeit.swiss (SECO) and cantonal URC websites. They can attend events and job fairs.
