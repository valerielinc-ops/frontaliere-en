Context

TL;DR - New Spitex tariffs in Ticino from April 2026 - Users pay 50 cents per 5 minutes, max 15 CHF/day - Aims to cover rising costs and promote sustainability - Growth in demand strains resources and budgets ## Key facts - New tariffs: 50 cents per 5 minutes of care, max 15 CHF/day - Effective date: April 1, 2026 - Annual revenue: Approximately 11 million CHF - Growth in care hours: Doubled over the past four years - Legislation approval: July 2024 - Exemptions: Beneficiaries of urgent services or AVS-AI benefits - Municipality example: Bellinzona saw a 30% cost increase in 5 years - Parliament approval: November 2024 Ticino Spitex: New Rates and Sector Pressure In Ticino, the home care sector, commonly known as Spitex, is undergoing a significant transformation. The recent introduction of new tariffs, effective from April 1, 2026, marks a crucial step toward ensuring the sustainability and quality of services in the medium and long term. According to the Council of State, this measure aims not only to cover rising costs but also to make beneficiaries of home care more responsible, introducing for the first time a systematic direct contribution from users. Currently, invoices for medical-therapeutic and basic services are generally covered by health insurance, supplemented by public funding. However, under the new rules, each user will contribute fifty cents every five minutes of care, up to a maximum of fifteen francs per day. For example, someone receiving an hour of daily assistance would pay about 6 francs per day, contributing to roughly 11 million francs in new annual revenue for the sector. This measure aims to contain costs and promote more sustainable resource management. Recent statistics show an impressive growth trend: hours of care provided have doubled over the past four years, with a 90% increase among private operators and a 65% rise in public services, such as in Lugano and some Mendrisio centers. This growth reflects efforts to keep citizens autonomous longer, avoiding early admissions to care facilities or hospitals. However, this increasing demand strains available resources, creating sustainability challenges even for larger municipalities. > Practical example: in the municipality of Bellinzona, the rise in home care requests has led to an estimated 30% increase in costs over the past five years. The demand for services increased by 20% just in 2023, complicating management and increasing the workload for social services. > Legislation: the new cantonal law, approved in July 2024, sets tariffs and user contributions, aiming to balance public and private funding. It also includes mechanisms for verification and oversight to prevent abuse or excessive contributions, respecting social equity principles. ⚠️ However, this strategy presents operational challenges: - Checklist for implementation: - update billing systems to include new contributions - train staff on new regulations - clearly communicate changes to beneficiaries - constantly monitor costs and revenues - establish advisory services to help users understand new tariffs > Comparing practical scenarios: a user needing 10 hours of assistance weekly, who previously paid around 70 francs, might now contribute about 30-35 francs weekly thanks to the direct contribution, easing public costs. Yet, the risk of reduced service demand due to new charges could lead to lower revenue for care providers, creating a chain effect on the sector’s sustainability. In conclusion, the new Spitex tariffs represent an important step toward a more sustainable and responsible model, but require careful operational and communicative management to ensure that the goal of keeping citizens autonomous longer is achieved without compromising service quality and availability.

The new tariff, of 50 cents per five minutes of assistance, applies to recipients of home care services, excluding those receiving supplementary AVS-AI benefits. Approved by the Ticino Parliament in the 2026 budget (deliberated in November 2024) and ratified by the Council of State in January 2025, this measure aims to curb cost increases and make citizens more responsible for the use of public and private resources. > Concrete example: a recipient receiving 2 hours of assistance daily (120 minutes) will pay about 2.00 CHF per day, or roughly 60 CHF per month, based on an active daily routine. This amount could pose a challenge for some families, especially in municipalities like Lugano or Bellinzona, where home care is highly requested. The process involves, once a care request is activated, the patient receiving a detailed monthly invoice, with the total amount calculated based on hours of assistance provided. Payment responsibility also extends to involved family members, reinforcing the idea of shared accountability. > The regulation specifies that beneficiaries of urgent services or assistance modes included in the individual care plan will be exempt from this tariff, to avoid penalizing emergency or critical situations. This exception aims to protect the most vulnerable groups and ensure prompt intervention without additional costs. > ⚠️ The main challenge for municipalities and Spitex organizations will be balancing service quality with financial sustainability, preventing user contributions from becoming overly burdensome. In some cases, the introduction of this tariff might lead to a reduction in requests, creating a barrier to access for home care in certain districts of Locarno or Mendrisio. > 📊 Comparing practical scenarios: currently, a municipality like Stabio or Ascona spends about 10 million CHF annually on home care.
