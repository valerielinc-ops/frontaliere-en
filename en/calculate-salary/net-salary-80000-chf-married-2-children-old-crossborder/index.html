Frequently asked

What is the net salary with CHF 80'000 gross as a cross-border worker?

An old cross-border worker earning CHF 80'000 gross nets approximately CHF 75'280 annually (CHF 6'273/month).

Which tax table applies to CHF 80'000 married?

Table B is the ordinary Ticino withholding tax for a Swiss resident: CHF 400 (0.50%) of gross pay. Swiss source withholding in this old cross-border worker scenario is CHF 400 (0.50%) of gross pay.

Is it better to live in Switzerland or commute with CHF 80'000?

On net pay, the cross-border worker is ahead by about CHF 16'800 a year (EUR 18.290) compared to a Swiss resident. Rent and cost of living are not included.

How net salary is calculated

With a gross annual income of CHF 80'000, the Swiss employer withholds mandatory social contributions of approximately CHF 10'320: AVS (5.3%), unemployment insurance (1.1%), accident insurance (0.7%), daily sickness allowance (0.8%), and occupational pension LPP. The Ticino withholding tax is calculated using table B, resulting in an effective rate of 0.5%.

Tax regime applied

As an old cross-border worker (Article 9 of the Italy-Switzerland agreement), your salary is taxable only in Switzerland through withholding tax: no Italian IRPEF is due on it. The overall effective rate is 0.5%.

Family situation impact

As a married person with 2 children, tax table B applies with reduced rates. Swiss family allowances amount to CHF 6'000/year.

Border distance zone

The status requires residence in a municipality on the official list, with territory at least partly inside the 20 km zone, and a return home that is in principle daily. The cantons of Ticino, Graubünden and Valais pay Italy a 40% compensation through tax year 2033: it does not change your net pay.

Indicative monthly budget

Monthly, a Swiss resident nets approximately CHF 4'873, while an Italian cross-border worker receives about EUR 6.830/month (at CHF/EUR 1.0887; TwelveData source, Firestore snapshot · observed 2026-11-03). The monthly gap is about CHF 1'400 in favour of the cross-border worker.
