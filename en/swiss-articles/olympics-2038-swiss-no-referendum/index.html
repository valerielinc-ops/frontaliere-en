2038 Olympics in Switzerland: no referendums, confirmation of 200 million (cross-border guide)

Di Valerie Linc · Published on 22 June 2026

The Federal Council has announced that there will be no popular vote on the Swiss bid for the 2038 Olympics. The confirmation of 200 million francs has passed to Parliament.

Contesto

In short - No federal referendum on the 2038 Olympics - Federal contribution of 200 million francs - Decision confirmed by the Federal Council - The candidature will be decentralized to the cantons ## Key facts - What : No federal popular vote on the 2038 Olympic candidature - When: Official announcement today - Where: Switzerland, decision of the Federal Council - Who: Federal Council, Parliament, UFSPO - Amount: 200 million francs from the Confederation The Federal Council announced today that in the process of approving the Swiss candidature for the 2038 Olympic and Paralympic Winter Games no referendum will be held at the federal level. The decision, already communicated in January, was reiterated during an official press conference, where Federal Councillor Martin Pfiser also confirmed that the Confederation will support the candidacy with a contribution of 200 million francs. The decree authorising this expenditure is now before Parliament. Pfiser explained that the failure to call the ballot depends on the fact that the requested credit is not far-reaching. In addition, he pointed out that in Switzerland, at the federal level, there is no financial referendum instrument. Dettagli operativi

Practical implications and future scenarios The absence of a federal referendum represents a strategic choice by the Swiss government, which aims to reduce political complications and facilitate more efficient preparation. From a practical point of view, this decision means that local authorities and citizens in the venues hosting the competitions will have a more direct and decision-making role. This approach aligns with the federalist character of Switzerland, where cantons and cities play an important role in event management. From a financial point of view, the 200 million francs made available by the Confederation represent only a part of the total budget. The main revenue will come from sponsorships and sales, but it is expected that private entities and international sponsors will also contribute to cover the remaining costs. The distribution of funds between the various areas of intervention will be decisive to ensure the success of the event. With this strategy, Switzerland is preparing to compete with other countries that have already submitted bids for the 2038 Olympics. The possibility of organising the games without a referendum, but with the decentralised involvement of cantons and municipalities, could also represent an innovative model for other international events. Finally, this choice could also influence future policy decisions regarding participation in major sporting events.

It is essential to monitor this phase, as any changes or integration requests could affect the plans. Interested citizens can follow the updates through the official websites of the institutions, such as the Swiss parliament and the UFSPO. Frequently Asked Questions
Why won't Switzerland hold a referendum on the 2038 candidacy?
The Federal Council has decided that the 200 million franc credit is not far-reaching and, in Switzerland, there is no financial referendum instrument at the federal level. The decision aims to facilitate the application, which will be decentralised.
What is the role of citizens in competition venues?
In Switzerland, local parliaments and the populations of the host areas will be able to express their opinion on decisions related to competitions, while national citizens will not vote at the federal level.
How will the costs of the event be covered?
The main expenses will be covered by private individuals, with income from sponsorships, merchandising, ticket sales and IOC contributions. The Confederation will contribute 200 million francs, subject to the availability of similar amounts from cantons and municipalities.
What are the next steps for the application?
The federal decree with the contribution of 200 million will be passed to Parliament in the coming months. If approved, Switzerland will be able to submit the dossier by 2027 and, if it meets the requirements of the IOC, will obtain the organisation without competition.
